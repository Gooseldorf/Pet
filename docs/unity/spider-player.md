# Spider Player

The spider uses a dynamic Rigidbody to traverse static, physically reachable `Traversable` surfaces, including walls, ceilings, curved geometry, and compatible seams. An attached body cannot leave a surface except through a jump and stops only at an unsupported edge. For closed convex colliders, the support normal follows the collider's closest-point distance field, so the spherical body smoothly rounds sharp outer edges without a speculative target surface. Nearby coplanar `BoxCollider` faces are resolved as one local patch across a gap no wider than the body radius; the aggregate's external boundary still follows its closest-point field, and wider gaps stay unsupported. Open and non-convex geometry retains physical face contacts and cannot create an artificial continuation beyond its boundary. A jump preserves tangential velocity and launches along the selected support normal. While airborne, the body falls under authored gravity, follows the camera's full orientation, accepts reduced camera-relative input, and reattaches when an eligible surface enters its search range. Moving and rotating supports, sprint, and web behaviour remain outside this slice.

`SpiderPlayerController` owns the one explicit physics tick. Its surface detector queries only physical sides of eligible static colliders, selects a proximity-weighted, mutually compatible group of contacts, and exposes the resulting read-only support state. A contact must be compatible with every member of the selected group so nearby opposing surfaces are never averaged together. Its locomotion motor moves tangent to that surface, holds the authored collider offset, and aligns the body up with the support normal while following the camera heading. Do not split those tightly ordered stages into independent MonoBehaviour callbacks without a new approved reason.

Movement input is interpreted in the body frame: the body forward follows the camera heading projected onto the attached surface, while lateral and reverse input remain strafe and reverse movement. Tune traversal and visual leg stepping through `SpiderConfig`; enable `SpiderSurfaceVisualizer` or `SpiderLegVisualizer` on the spider prefab when adjusting a traversal environment or authored offsets. The visualizers render diagnostics at runtime and do not extend the public surface-state API.

The public surface state is the integration boundary for systems that need the body's support. It reports whether the body is attached, the blended support point and normal, and the contributing colliders. Consumers observe it and must not own body movement, body-surface sampling, or controller tick order. Visual legs use their own per-leg static-surface queries, selecting a reachable point and normal for each foot independently of the body's attached collider; they do not feed forces, orientation, or attachment back to the body.

The preserved boundary is explicit runtime spawn: gameplay startup creates the spider and its camera rig, then the rig binds to authored follow and look targets on the spawned spider. Keep camera ownership in the camera slice and do not replace that binding with scene searches or scene-owned target references. The world-space spherical orbit can traverse its complete vertical range; its Aim stage preserves orientation continuity by rolling the frame through either pole instead of clamping or snapping upright.

The current spider scope is single-player. Do not infer networking support; evaluate authority and synchronization only when multiplayer is introduced or requested.
